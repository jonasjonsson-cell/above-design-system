# Graphics & the 5th element

## The 5th element — the dash

The dash is the bar from the ā mark, used as a layout shape.

<div style={{ display: 'flex', gap: 24, alignItems: 'center', margin: '24px 0' }}>
  <Dash width={240} />
  <Dash width={240} fill="var(--primary-green-01)" />
</div>

- **Original shape.** Use the dash to create distinct layouts: fill it with an image, mask part of a background image, or use the bold shape itself in the layout.
- **Interpretation.** Go all out with custom graphics using the dash as a starting point — 3D, bent, moved — as long as it still reads as the dash.
- **Start flat.** Establish the dash as a known graphic sign before illustrating it in 3D space. Watch that it doesn't interfere with product mockups and renders.

## Graphics

- **Heavy, geometric.** Graphics are heavier than the light typeface, for contrast. Geometric, precise, regular, symmetrical — curved and angular shapes on the same symbol ("sharp and round corners").
- **Bold and small, or large and fine.**
- **Avoid thin graphics.** Thin outline icons (a thin arrow in a pill, a thin plus in a circle) don't contrast enough with the type.

## Structural graphics

Thin lines or patterns used as a tool — to structure a layout or give context (dividers under section labels, grids behind a model). They organise; they don't decorate.
